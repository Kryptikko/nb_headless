# The Three Axes at Group Level
Upward–Downward (Power & Status Structure)
At the individual level this is dominance vs. submission. At the group level, this becomes:

How steep is the status hierarchy? — A group with a very "UP" center of gravity is one where authority, assertiveness, and task leadership are the dominant cultural values. Think military units, high-performance trading floors, certain startup cultures
Flatness vs. verticality — Groups centered near the midpoint on this axis have diffuse authority and more democratic process
The key group-level phenomenon Bales documented is status consolidation: early on, the UD dimension is fluid; within ~4 sessions a dominance hierarchy crystallizes and becomes self-reinforcing. The group as a system then actively resists reordering

The critical insight: once a hierarchy forms, it takes on structural inertia independent of the individuals. Replace the dominant person and the group often re-installs someone in the same UP role within weeks — the role persists even when the person changes.

Forward–Backward (Task vs. Emotional/Expressive Orientation)
At the individual level: task-focused vs. emotionally expressive. At the group level:

F-dominant groups are instrumentally oriented — meetings have agendas, relationships are functional, sentiment is suppressed in favor of output. Corporate task forces, academic committees
B-dominant groups organize around belonging, feeling, and interpersonal meaning — therapy groups, certain religious communities, tight friendship circles
The tension Bales identified: almost every group experiences a F/B oscillation. Groups that push too far Forward accumulate emotional debt (suppressed conflict, unmet relational needs) that eventually erupts backward. Groups that sit too far Backward lose productivity and often fragment through interpersonal drama

This oscillation was one of Bales' most important findings — the FB axis at group level is inherently dynamic, not a stable equilibrium. He observed cycles of task focus → emotional buildup → expressive release → re-task-orientation happening within single sessions, not just over weeks.


# Polarization vs. Unification — The Group-Level Phenomenon Bales Considered Most Dangerous

This is where group personality analysis diverges most sharply from individual analysis. A group can have a center of gravity in UPF space but simultaneously be polarized — with two dense subclusters pulling in opposite directions.
Bales identified several polarization patterns:

## UPF vs. DNB polarization — The classic authority/rebellion split. A dominant coalition pushing task-positive-dominant values generates a reactive counter-coalition in the diagonally opposite space. This is extremely common and maps onto what we'd recognize as management vs. disengaged workers, orthodoxy vs. reformers, etc.

## UNF vs. UPB polarization — Two dominant factions, one task-oriented-hostile and one dominant-but-warm. This produces vicious internal competition dressed up in different moral languages. Common in political parties, academic departments, high-stakes teams.

## The unification condition — Bales found that groups under external threat (a common enemy, a shared crisis) tend to rapidly compress their polarization. The field collapses toward a unified center temporarily. This explains wartime social cohesion, crisis team performance, and why some groups only function well under pressure.
